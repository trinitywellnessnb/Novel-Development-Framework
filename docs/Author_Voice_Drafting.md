# Drafting in the author's voice

Use this guide to make choices before and during an authorized fiction draft. Run the [Humanizer prose pass](Humanizer_Prose_Pass.md) afterward. These are editorial tools, not permission to write, import evidence, promote canon, or publish.

## Project application

Attach this guidance to the existing [Writing Context Brief](../09_Tools/Templates/Writing_Context_Brief.md) instead of creating a parallel writing or knowledge system. Keep this upstream copy neutral; put author-specific references only in the appropriate authorized project workspace. Leave the voice profile provisional when samples are unavailable. Existing writing approval, canon, and publication gates still apply.

## 1. Brief the voice and scene

Choose the smallest mode that serves the request: new scene, bounded rewrite, transition, alternate approach, or line edit. Record what may change and what must remain intact. Name the viewpoint and tense, what the viewpoint knows and cannot know, what the character wants now, the pressure on that want, and what should change for the character or reader by the end. Note what must stay unsaid. Mark working assumptions separately from approved material.

For an author-voice reference, use short, authorized samples the author regards as representative of this book, viewpoint, and kind of scene. Analyze how the author handles rhythm under pressure, observation, figurative language, dialogue, interior thought, and changes in narrative distance. Capture two to four actionable observations, each with a source anchor and a reason. Label what the author has stated versus what you inferred. Where possible, reserve a separate authorized passage to check whether the observations generalize. Do not copy sample phrases into new prose. If samples are missing, use stated preferences and mark any proposed style choices as provisional.

A compact note is enough: **Mode and limits / POV and distance / want and pressure / intended reader effect / withheld information / voice evidence and anchors / any decision needed.** Use the existing project brief and approval route rather than making this another required form.

## 2. Draft the beat before polishing

- Start where the reader has something to follow: a task, decision, encounter, or thought under pressure. A setting-first opening or quiet transition is valid when it does real work. Do not treat every beat as a full scene or require every scene to end on a cliffhanger.
- Filter detail through the viewpoint. In a close passage, choose the detail this character would notice for a reason, and let their judgments color the sentence. Move closer or farther on purpose. Do not enforce close third in an established first-person, distant, or omniscient passage.
- Make dialogue speakable and specific to the relationship. A speaker may stall, restart, dodge, or state a feeling plainly. Use an action beat or the sentence itself when it conveys more than an adverb or a vague tone description. Keep plain tags, direct feeling, and earned repetition when they work.
- Follow the pressure point through what someone does, decides, feels, or fails to do. Preserve the turn; vary the rhythm around it. Let a changed situation, purposeful silence, or earned image finish the scene rather than explaining its moral. Don't make every argument end in agreement or every scene settle back into calm.

## 3. Review in separate passes

For substantial or pivotal prose, read the finished passage once for reader experience: where is the first question, what feels earned, where does attention drift, and what changes by the end? Then check continuity, viewpoint knowledge, reveal timing, and scene causality separately from line quality. A fresh reviewer can help if available and authorized; otherwise do separate reads. Quote or point to a specific passage for each material finding. Simulated reactions are hypotheses, not feedback from real readers.

Fix the largest approved issue first, then apply the Humanizer pass. Flag unauthorized structural or canon changes separately. Compare repeated sentence shapes, gestures, or endings across authorized nearby scenes only when it helps; judge the pattern in context, not by a quota. A line that carries the author's intent should survive. Normally, bring unresolved taste or direction choices back to the author after at most two review-and-revision rounds unless they request more.

For a critical beat with genuinely open direction, compare two authorized takes that differ in one meaningful choice, such as distance or point of entry. Keep the story facts fixed. Explain the reader-effect tradeoff and present the choice without treating either take as approved.

## Research decisions

- [Fiction Prose Style](https://github.com/unkarelian/fiction-prose-style/tree/49409a4c1b3ec31d9efc9d27247acc31e0726801): use its viewpoint-sensitive texture, tolerance for earned emotional language, and fresh-reader review method. Its stylistic bans, example corpus, detector, and numerical baselines are not project standards.
- [Creative Writing Skills](https://github.com/haowjy/creative-writing-skills/tree/ced41ba9964e9cf50bcae2707b1e3fa0407c8a89): use scoped drafting modes, evidence-based author voice notes, and separate review lenses. Do not install its agent stack or replace this project's approval, archive, or canon workflow.
- [LLM Tolkien](https://github.com/jeremyarancio/llm-tolkien/tree/e9575364893196d53892a483dd71dbb397a93186) and [Writes Like You](https://github.com/deven367/writes-like-you/tree/101a4b906f3caf2d62a578efdb33a277fcf07a44): keep one practical lesson from their training approach: match style references to the intended kind of writing. Use a few author-authorized samples as in-context references instead of importing Tolkien text, bulk-exporting private notes, or training a model. Do not install dependencies, download weights, call external judging services, or export manuscript data for this workflow.
