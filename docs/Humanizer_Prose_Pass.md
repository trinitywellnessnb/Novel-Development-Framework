# Humanizer prose pass

For decisions before and during drafting, use [Drafting in the author's voice](Author_Voice_Drafting.md). This pass is for prose that already exists.

Use this last-pass checklist for AI-assisted fiction or a line edit the author has already authorized. It is an editorial aid, not an AI detector, a new writing authority, or permission to inspect or rewrite anything. Start with the author's samples and the scene's voice. Keep purposeful repetition, fragments, parallelism, metaphor, and punctuation when they earn their place. Do not sand character voices into the same narrator.

## Project guardrails

Use the author’s approved voice samples, viewpoint, narrative scale, and house style. In this public framework, examples must be fictional and project-neutral. This pass belongs after authorized drafting and does not replace writing briefs, continuity review, or publication authorization.

Never change plot facts, event order, viewpoint knowledge, approved canon, invented names or terms, or the intent of dialogue. Do not add a concrete detail unless it is already supported by the authorized draft or reference material; flag anything that requires an author choice. Leave verbatim quotes, transcripts, historical drafts, and archival records unchanged.

## What to revise

- **Staged contrast and false depth.** Cut a “not X but Y” setup when X is a straw man. Keep a real correction or contrast. Replace slogan-like narration with what the character notices, does, chooses, or risks.
- **Redundant endings and run-ups.** Enter on the first useful action or thought. Cut a dramatic one-line closer if it only explains what the scene has already shown. Keep a short line that changes the stakes or lands an intentional beat.
- **Mechanical rhythm.** Look for forced sets of three, the same sentence opening in a row, or dash-heavy clauses. Keep meaningful parallelism; otherwise let the order of events and the character's breath set the cadence. Avoid long dashes in new wording unless the author’s style calls for them.
- **Generic intensity.** Replace vague grandeur, abstract metaphors, and significance labels with a supported physical detail, decision, consequence, or simpler verb. Don't invent facts or flatten intentional grandeur.
- **Expository dialogue and restatement.** Cut explanations the speaker and listener both know unless the repetition serves a purpose in the scene. Keep evasions, interruptions, subtext, and gaps in understanding. Remove assistant greetings, progress narration, and follow-up offers from manuscript text.
- **Weak tells in context.** A single stock word, hedge, passive verb, or punctuation choice is not proof anything is wrong. Edit only when it obscures meaning, repeats a pattern, or breaks the established voice.

## How to run the pass

1. Read the whole authorized passage and enough approved context to understand the scene. Mark where the prose loses specificity, repeats itself, or falls out of voice.
2. Rewrite the smallest span that fixes the issue. Prefer the author's idiom over the checklist. If a fix would change meaning, continuity, structure, or characterization, leave it and flag it separately.
3. Compare before and after for point of view, action sequence, certainty, dialogue intent, proper nouns, and deliberate ambiguity. Read the new prose for rhythm. Return the edited passage or requested diff; report out-of-scope changes as suggestions only.

## Neutral example

Before: “The latch rattled in her hand. It wasn't just a warning. Beyond the door, boots scraped the stone. The danger was closer now.”

After: “The latch rattled in her hand. Beyond the door, boots scraped the stone.”

If the final line instead tells the reader a new fact about the approaching threat, retain that fact in the rewrite.
